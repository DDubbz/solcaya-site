Music for private estates

A private estate is not a venue — it is a home. The music should feel the same way: composed for the space, shaped to the guests, and built to carry the evening from arrival to last dance without a single jarring transition. The studio builds bespoke DJ mixes that do exactly that.

Why estates are different from venues

A venue is built for events. The acoustics are managed, the sound system is installed, the layout is designed for crowds. A private estate is the opposite — it is a residence first. The music has to work within someone's home: their rooms, their neighbours, their atmosphere. That changes everything about how the sound should be composed.

Space shapes sound

Hardwood floors, high ceilings, open-plan living, marble walls — every surface changes how music sounds. A mix that sounds perfect in a club can feel harsh in a residential space. The studio composes with the listening environment in mind: selecting tracks that sound warm and full in large rooms without overwhelming conversation, building energy without blowing out the neighbours.

The three arcs of an estate event

Most private estate events follow a similar shape. The music should move with it.

1. Arrival and cocktails

Guests are greeting each other, finding their bearings, settling in. The music should be present but not demanding — warm, textured, and paced so conversation flows naturally. Think deep house, soul, or curated downtempo. The goal is atmosphere, not attention.

2. The main event

Dinner, speeches, the dance floor — whatever the centrepiece is, the music shifts to support it. This is where the studio's hand-composed pacing matters most: building energy deliberately, cresting at the right moment, and holding the floor without letting the energy drop between songs. A playlist cannot do this. Only a mix can.

3. Wind-down and after-party

As the evening shifts from peak to close, the music should shift with it. The studio composes the tail of the mix to bring the energy down without killing the mood — keeping the warmth, letting the tempo ease, and ending on the note you want people to leave with.

Discretion is non-negotiable

At a private estate, discretion is part of the brief. The studio delivers white-label as standard — no Solcaya branding, no attribution, just the mix. An NDA is available on request and included as standard on Solcaya-tier commissions. The neighbours should hear great music. They should never know where it came from.

What the consultation covers for estates

The private taste consultation is where the studio learns about your space, your guests, and your evening. Key questions include: What rooms will the event occupy? What is the sound system — installed speakers, a DJ setup, portable PA? What time does the event start, and are there noise considerations? Who are the guests — ages, backgrounds, musical leanings? The answers shape the mix: track selection, volume profile, energy curve, and delivery format.

Delivery formats for estates

  • MP3 — universal playback, any device
  • WAV — mastered audio for installed sound systems
  • Stems (Caya and Solcaya tiers) — individual track control for your sound engineer
  • Clean edits — available on request for family or corporate events

How far in advance should I book?

Sol commissions require seven business days from brief and deposit to delivery. Caya is five business days with priority handling. For Solcaya — multi-hour or multi-mix event suites — the timeline is agreed during consultation. For estates, the studio recommends booking at least two weeks in advance to allow time for a thorough consultation and at least one revision.
